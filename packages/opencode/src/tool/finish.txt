End your turn by reporting the outcome. You MUST call this tool exactly once at the end of every reply — it is the only way to complete your turn.

Every user message starts a task, even one that needs no tools. Greetings, questions, and conversational or explanatory answers all end with a finish call; answering without using any tools does not remove the requirement. Producing text is never enough on its own — ending your message alone does not complete the turn. The session keeps running until you call finish.

Pass `reason` and `result`. `reason` is required and must be one of:
- `success`: the task completed and the result is usable. Call this whenever your own work is done, even if a background subagent you launched is still running — its notification wakes the session afterwards.
- `waiting_for_subagent`: your own task is not finished and you have no independent work left this turn while background subagents you launched keep running, so you are yielding it. Only valid while at least one of those subagents is still running; their notification wakes the session when one of them ends. A subagent may yield the same way, but only for background subagents it launched itself — its job stays running until they report — and with nothing of its own in flight it must deliver `success` or `failure` instead.
- `subagent_wait`: progress depends on another subagent and the orchestrator should handle that dependency.
- `failure`: the task could not be completed successfully.

Pass `result` as a concise summary of what you did or answered. These are silent protocol signals, not something to announce to the user — do not say that you are finishing; just call it as the last step of your reply.

Retrying finish does not skip review. When the medium pass budget is exhausted, the result is delivered as `budget-exhausted`, never as an approval. Medium and thorough dispatches that cannot run end as `review-blocked`, and a child's unreviewed handoff ends as `review-pending`. At medium and thorough effort, finish requires an explicit Approved review of that work from the Review subagent (dispatch it with `subagent_type: "review"` and `background: false`); at fast effort no review is required. Whether an independent review is required depends on your effort policy (fast/medium/thorough), and `finish` is the only enforcement checkpoint: when the effective level requires an independent review that has not happened, the call is declined with recoverable feedback. Repeated `finish` calls never waive a required review — the decision is based only on the persisted review state for this turn. At `fast`, no review is required and the result is honestly labelled unreviewed unless an independent review actually happened. At `medium`, deliver after at least one clean synchronous `review` pass (`subagent_type: "review"`, `background: false`); only blocking findings or work landed after the last review force another pass, bounded by two passes, after which `finish` delivers with an honest exhausted status. At `thorough`, `finish` keeps declining until a clean current review exists, or is blocked as `review-blocked` when the reviewer genuinely cannot run — never as approval.

You may pass `validation.checks` declaring validation evidence for this result: one entry per check with `category` (syntax, smoke, tests, typecheck, parity), `status` (passed, failed, not-run, not-applicable), and a short `detail`. finish runs nothing itself — it records your declaration against the gate derived from your effort level, and missing or failed evidence is reported as incomplete/failed, never as passed.

Call it once, after any other tool calls have completed. Do not call it to report partial progress, and do not use it to ask a question — use the question tool for questions and keep working otherwise.

When finish succeeds, it makes a best-effort attempt to close any remaining open todos (pending or in_progress) for this task as cancelled, leaving a clean terminal todo state when possible. This cleanup does not block task completion — if todo closure fails, finish still succeeds and the failure is logged. This is a safety net — you must still explicitly mark individual todo items completed during execution rather than relying on finish to clean up. A `waiting_for_subagent` finish is not a completion: it keeps the todo list, the review requirement, and the terminal result metadata untouched so the wait does not consume state the eventual finish needs, and it is only accepted while a background subagent of yours is running — a subagent that yields must have launched that subagent itself — and once the last one ends, deliver with `success` or `failure`.
